Bluetooth¶

Extension's Features¶

  • Scan for Bluetooth Low Energy (LE) devices, with filters on services, names and advertised data, and read their advertisements
  • Connect to LE devices as a client and discover, read, write and subscribe to their GATT services, characteristics and descriptors
  • Run a GATT server of your own, advertise it, and answer the read and write requests of connected devices
  • Scan for, connect to and host Bluetooth Classic (RFCOMM) serial connections
  • Pair with devices, list paired devices and reconnect to a device you saw before without scanning
  • Ask for the Bluetooth permissions and query what the current platform supports

Introduction¶

This extension gives you access to Bluetooth on Windows, macOS, iOS and Android through one API. Bluetooth Low Energy works on all four platforms. Bluetooth Classic works on Windows, Android and macOS; on iOS bluetooth_feature_is_supported returns false for BluetoothFeature.Classic and the Classic functions that start something fail with BluetoothError.NotSupported. The extension requires GameMaker 2026.0 or later.

Not every platform can do everything: advertising, for example, is limited to a subset of the advertisement on Apple platforms. You should call bluetooth_feature_is_supported after bluetooth_initialize to find out what the current platform and adapter can do, rather than checking os_type.

You must call bluetooth_initialize once before anything else, and on Android and Apple platforms ask for permission with bluetooth_permission_request before you scan, connect or advertise. Getting Started walks through a first scan and connection, and Platform Setup lists what each platform needs from your project.

Every function that can fail reports it in one of two places. A function returns a BluetoothError straight away for anything that can be rejected before the operation starts, such as an invalid handle, a missing permission or the radio being off, and when that value is not Ok its callback is never called. You can read the reason with bluetooth_last_error_message. Once the operation has started, the callback is called exactly once, and its first two arguments are an error_code and a message describing the outcome. The functions that return a handle are the exception to the first rule: the two connect functions, bluetooth_device_from_id and the three find functions return 0 when they fail.

Events belong to the call that starts what they are about. A scan, a connection, a subscription, an advertisement and each server take the functions to call for their events as arguments, such as on_found or on_disconnected, and keep them until they end; on_stopped or on_disconnected is the last event of each. The only global callback is the adapter state callback, set with bluetooth_set_callback_state_changed. Starting something that is already running returns BluetoothError.Busy, and stopping something that is not running returns BluetoothError.Ok and does nothing.

What the game ends fires no event: a stop or a disconnect you ask for takes effect when the call returns, and no on_stopped or on_disconnected is called for it. When the radio is turned off, everything running ends and reports BluetoothError.BluetoothDisabled through those callbacks, and nothing restarts when the radio comes back (see BluetoothState).

Devices, connections and GATT attributes are all identified by handles, which are numbers that the extension gives you. Byte data that arrives from a device is held by the extension and given to you as a value handle and a size, which you copy into a buffer of your own with bluetooth_value_fetch, or with the GML helpers bluetooth_value_to_buffer and bluetooth_value_append that ship with the extension.
